# Mechanical Seal Failure Analysis and Reliability Guide (Site SOP MS-07)

SYNTHETIC DEMONSTRATION DOCUMENT. Written for the PlantSentinel demo; not a real site procedure.

## 1 Purpose

This SOP explains how to read a failed mechanical seal, how to decide when repeated seal
failures need a root cause analysis, and what to check before fitting a new seal.

## 2 When an RCA is mandatory

An RCA is mandatory when a criticality A pump has two or more seal failures within 6 months,
or when the mean time between seal failures falls below 90 days. Replacing the seal without an
RCA in these cases is not permitted.

<!-- page: 2 -->
## 3 Reading the failed seal

Heat checking (radial cracks) on the silicon carbide face, carbon face blistering, or
discoloured O-rings: the faces overheated. Causes are loss of flush, dry running, or running
below minimum flow. Check the flush orifice and the flush line first.

Wide or eccentric wear track on the faces: the shaft is not running true. Causes are
misalignment, bent shaft, worn bearings or pipe strain. Check alignment, run-out and bearings.

Chipped face edges: vibration or cavitation. Check vibration spectrum and suction conditions.

Hardened, cracked or swollen O-rings with an otherwise good face: chemical incompatibility or
over-temperature of the elastomer.

<!-- page: 3 -->
## 4 Overheating and seal life

Seal faces run on a film of liquid only a few micrometres thick. If the seal chamber
temperature rises, the film flashes to vapour, the faces run dry and overheat, and leakage
follows. A seal gland that runs hot, a hot flush line, or a flush line that is cool when it
should be hot (no flow) all indicate a flush problem.

Repeated overheating-related seal failures on the same pump almost always have a cause outside
the seal itself. Typical findings are a partially blocked flush orifice, misalignment after
motor work, or a pump operated at low flow.

## 5 Recommended corrective actions for repeat seal failures

1. Inspect and clean or replace the Plan 11 flush orifice; confirm flow by feeling a warm
   flush line during operation.
2. Check shaft alignment and correct to the OEM tolerance. Check for soft foot and pipe strain.
3. Measure vibration at bearing housings and coupling; investigate any value above 4.5 mm/s RMS.
4. Check bearing condition and lubricating oil; replace bearings if noisy or if vibration is high.
5. Confirm the pump is operated above minimum continuous flow.
6. Keep the failed seal and send it with the RCA report.

## 6 Safety

Follow the OEM manual for isolation, depressurising and draining before opening any seal
chamber. Apply LOTO and verify zero energy. Gas test before breaking containment. Treat seal
chamber contents as hot and flammable.

## 7 Planning information

Seal replacement with alignment check: 8 hours, two fitters. RCA inspection (orifice,
alignment, vibration): additional 3 hours. Priority: High for criticality A pumps with a
repeat failure.
