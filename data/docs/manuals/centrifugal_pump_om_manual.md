# CP-200 Series Centrifugal Process Pump - Operation and Maintenance Manual

SYNTHETIC DEMONSTRATION DOCUMENT. Written for the PlantSentinel demo. It does not describe a
real manufacturer's product and must not be used for real maintenance.

Applies to: CP-200 series single-stage, end-suction, back-pull-out centrifugal pumps in
hydrocarbon service, fitted with a single cartridge mechanical seal and an API Plan 11 seal
flush. Site tags covered by this manual include the P-100 series process pumps.

## 1 Scope

This manual covers safe isolation, operation, troubleshooting, mechanical seal replacement,
shaft alignment and bearing lubrication. Read Section 2 (Safety) before any maintenance task.

<!-- page: 2 -->
## 2 Safety

WARNING: Before any maintenance, apply lockout/tagout (LOTO) to the motor starter and verify
zero energy by attempting a local start. The pump must not be able to rotate.

WARNING: Close and lock the suction and discharge isolation valves. Depressurise the casing
through the vent and drain the casing to the closed drain system before opening any joint.
Hydrocarbon liquid and vapour may be released.

WARNING: Pump casing, seal gland and bearing housing surfaces can exceed 80 degC in normal
service. Allow the pump to cool to below 50 degC or wear heat-resistant gloves.

CAUTION: Carry out a gas test before breaking containment in a classified area and keep a
hot-work permit closed unless the area is confirmed gas free.

Minimum PPE: safety helmet, safety glasses, chemical-resistant gloves, flame-retardant
coveralls and safety footwear.

## 3 Technical data

Rated flow: 120 m3/h. Rated head: 85 m. Speed: 2950 rpm. Driver: 45 kW induction motor.

Mechanical seal: single cartridge seal, carbon versus silicon carbide faces, seal part number
CS-45-SIC. Seal flush: API Plan 11 (discharge to seal chamber through an orifice of 3 mm).

Bearing housing temperature: normal below 70 degC; alarm at 82 degC; trip at 90 degC.
Seal gland temperature: normal below 60 degC above pumped-fluid temperature rise; a gland that
is too hot to touch indicates loss of flush or dry running.

Shaft alignment tolerance (coupling, at 2950 rpm): parallel offset 0.05 mm maximum; angular
0.05 mm per 100 mm maximum.

Bearing lubrication: ISO VG 68 mineral turbine oil, oil-bath with constant-level oiler.

<!-- page: 4 -->
## 4 Operation

Never run the pump dry. Dry running destroys the mechanical seal faces within seconds because
the faces depend on the pumped liquid for cooling and lubrication.

Do not operate below the minimum continuous flow of 30 m3/h. Operation at low flow raises
liquid temperature in the casing and seal chamber and causes recirculation and vibration.

Keep suction pressure above the NPSH required. Cavitation causes vibration and pressure
pulsation that open the seal faces momentarily and shorten seal life.

Before start-up, confirm the seal flush line and orifice are open and the casing is fully
vented and primed.

## 5 Troubleshooting

### 5.1 Mechanical seal leakage

Probable causes, in order of frequency in hydrocarbon service:
(a) Seal face overheating from loss of flush flow: blocked Plan 11 orifice or closed flush
valve. Look for heat checking (fine radial cracks) on the hard face and carbon face blistering.
(b) Dry running or running with the casing not primed.
(c) Shaft misalignment or excessive vibration at the coupling, which causes face separation and
uneven wear. Check alignment whenever seal failures repeat.
(d) Cavitation or operation below minimum flow.
(e) Incorrect installation: setting clips not removed, faces contaminated, O-rings damaged.

### 5.2 Pump overheating

High casing or seal gland temperature is caused by running against a closed discharge valve,
operation below minimum flow, or loss of seal flush. High bearing housing temperature is caused
by low oil level, wrong or degraded oil, over-greasing, or misalignment loading the bearings.

### 5.3 Repeated seal failures

If the same pump has more than two seal failures within 12 months, do not simply replace the
seal again. Raise a root cause analysis (RCA) and check, as a minimum: flush orifice and line,
shaft alignment, shaft run-out at the seal (0.05 mm TIR maximum), bearing condition and
vibration levels.

## 6 Mechanical seal replacement

Isolate the pump as described in Section 2 before starting.

1. Remove the coupling spacer and record the as-found alignment readings.
2. Disconnect the seal flush line. Inspect the Plan 11 orifice for blockage and record findings.
3. Fit the cartridge setting clips before loosening the seal sleeve set screws.
4. Remove the back-pull-out assembly and withdraw the cartridge seal.
5. Inspect the old seal faces for heat checking, chipping and uneven wear patterns and keep the
   failed seal for the RCA.
6. Check shaft run-out at the seal location: 0.05 mm TIR maximum.
7. Install the new cartridge seal (part CS-45-SIC) with new O-rings (kit OR-45-V). Tighten gland
   nuts evenly to 25 N.m.
8. Remove the setting clips after the sleeve set screws are tightened. Running with clips
   fitted destroys the seal.
9. Reinstall the assembly, carry out shaft alignment (Section 7) and reconnect the flush line.
10. Prime and vent the casing fully before start-up. Monitor the gland for leakage and
    temperature for the first 30 minutes.

Typical duration: 6 to 8 hours for two fitters, excluding isolation and permit time.

Materials: cartridge seal CS-45-SIC; O-ring kit OR-45-V; gland gasket GG-45; seal flush orifice
OF-3 (replace if eroded or blocked).

## 7 Shaft alignment

Use a laser or dial-indicator alignment tool. Align with the pump at ambient temperature.

1. Check for soft foot at each motor foot; correct any reading above 0.05 mm with shims.
2. Measure parallel offset and angularity at the coupling.
3. Correct to within the tolerance in Section 3: parallel 0.05 mm, angular 0.05 mm per 100 mm.
4. Record final readings on the work order.

Misalignment above tolerance is a common hidden cause of repeated seal and bearing failures and
of vibration at the coupling end.

<!-- page: 8 -->
## 8 Bearing lubrication

Check the constant-level oiler daily. Change the oil every 4000 running hours or every 6
months, whichever is first, or immediately if the oil is milky or dark.

Use ISO VG 68 turbine oil only. Do not overfill: oil above the oiler level increases churning
and bearing temperature.

If bearing housing temperature exceeds the alarm value (82 degC), check oil level and condition,
then vibration. Replace the bearings if vibration exceeds 4.5 mm/s RMS or if noise is present.
